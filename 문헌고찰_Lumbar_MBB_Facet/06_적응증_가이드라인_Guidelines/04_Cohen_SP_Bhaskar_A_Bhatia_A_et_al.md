### [보통] Cohen SP, Bhaskar A, Bhatia A, et al. Consensus practice guidelines on interventions for lumbar facet joint pain from a multispecialty, international working group. Reg Anesth Pain Med 2020;45(6):424-467 — the main counterweight to ASIPP, and the one most likely to generate audience discussion.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION (PMID recalled as 32245841 — verify). Recalled positions, in rough order of my confidence: (1) no history/exam finding reliably identifies facet pain, with paraspinal tenderness cited as possibly the least-bad single candidate but still weak; (2) the working group did NOT mandate a universal number of blocks or a universal relief threshold — instead it framed the choice of 0 vs 1 vs 2 blocks and 50% vs 80% as a function of local prevalence, cost structure, and practice setting, explicitly acknowledging the cost-effectiveness argument for fewer blocks; (3) recommends AGAINST intra-articular facet injection as a prognostic test before RFA (the medial branch is the target of RFA, so an IA block does not test the same neural pathway); (4) recommends against RFA with no prior positive block. This 'thresholds are a policy choice, not a biological constant' stance is the sharpest divergence from ASIPP's fixed dual-block/80% rule. Companion cervical document: Hurley RW, Adams MCB, Barad M, et al. Reg Anesth Pain Med 2022;47(1):3-59 (PMID recalled as 34764220 — verify).

### [보통] The 50% vs 80% threshold trade-off, stated plainly: raising the threshold raises RFA success rate per treated patient but shrinks the eligible pool and raises cost per success.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION — this is the synthesis, and it is the framing I would build the pathway slide around. At >=80% dual-block criterion (ASIPP/SIS), reported RFA success is high (Dreyfuss 2000: 60% with >=90% relief at 12 months; Cohen 2010 dual-block arm: ~64% at 3 months) but many patients are screened out. At >=50% single-block criterion (used by MINT and by many European and cost-constrained systems), the treated population is larger and more heterogeneous and reported success rates fall toward the 33-39% range seen in Cohen 2010's 0- and 1-block arms. Neither threshold is 'correct' — they encode different utility functions (maximize per-patient benefit vs. maximize population throughput per dollar). ASIPP/SIS resolve this toward 80%; Cohen 2020 explicitly declines to resolve it; MINT used the permissive end and got a null result. Present all three positions.

### [보통] Intra-articular facet injection should NOT be used as the prognostic test before RFA — a mechanistic point that resolves a common practice error.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION but mechanistically self-evident and, as I recall, explicit in both the Cohen 2020 consensus and Medicare policy. RFA lesions the MEDIAL BRANCH; therefore the anesthetic test that predicts RFA response must anesthetize the medial branch. An intra-articular injection tests the joint, not the nerve, and a positive IA response does not establish that denervating the medial branch will help. This also explains why ASIPP and the LCDs treat MBB and IA injection as non-interchangeable despite both being 'facet blocks,' and why IA facet injection tends to receive the weakest evidence grade of the four modalities in ASIPP's tables. Practical corollary: IA injection's legitimate roles are as a therapeutic intervention in its own right and as an alternative diagnostic block — not as the gateway to RFA.
