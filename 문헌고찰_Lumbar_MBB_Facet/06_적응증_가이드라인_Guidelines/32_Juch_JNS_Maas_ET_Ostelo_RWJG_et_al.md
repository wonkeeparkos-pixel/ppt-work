### [보통] The 50% vs 80% threshold trade-off, stated plainly: raising the threshold raises RFA success rate per treated patient but shrinks the eligible pool and raises cost per success.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION — this is the synthesis, and it is the framing I would build the pathway slide around. At >=80% dual-block criterion (ASIPP/SIS), reported RFA success is high (Dreyfuss 2000: 60% with >=90% relief at 12 months; Cohen 2010 dual-block arm: ~64% at 3 months) but many patients are screened out. At >=50% single-block criterion (used by MINT and by many European and cost-constrained systems), the treated population is larger and more heterogeneous and reported success rates fall toward the 33-39% range seen in Cohen 2010's 0- and 1-block arms. Neither threshold is 'correct' — they encode different utility functions (maximize per-patient benefit vs. maximize population throughput per dollar). ASIPP/SIS resolve this toward 80%; Cohen 2020 explicitly declines to resolve it; MINT used the permissive end and got a null result. Present all three positions.

### [보통] MINT (Juch et al., JAMA 2017): three RCTs, ~681 patients, RF denervation added to a standardized exercise program produced no clinically important improvement in pain at 3 months.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION. Juch JNS, Maas ET, Ostelo RWJG, et al. 'Effect of Radiofrequency Denervation on Pain Intensity Among Patients With Chronic Low Back Pain: The Mint Randomized Clinical Trials.' JAMA 2017;318(1):68-81 (PMID recalled 28672319 — verify). Recalled design: three parallel pragmatic RCTs conducted in the Netherlands in patients with chronic LBP presumed to arise from facet joints, SI joints, or a combination, with roughly 251 / 228 / 202 patients in the three trials (~681 total). All arms received a standardized exercise program; the intervention arm added RF denervation. Primary outcome: NRS pain intensity at 3 months. Recalled result for the facet trial: a between-group difference of roughly -0.18 NRS points (95% CI approximately -0.76 to 0.40), not statistically significant and far below any minimal clinically important difference. Overall conclusion as recalled: RF denervation added to a standardized exercise program resulted in either no improvement or no clinically important improvement.
