### [보통] ASIPP 2020 diagnostic criterion: dual (comparative) medial branch blocks with >=80% pain relief PLUS ability to perform previously painful movements, each block's relief lasting a duration concordant with the local anesthetic used.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION. The '>=80% relief with ability to perform previously painful movements' phrasing is ASIPP's signature wording and appears across Manchikanti's guideline series — I recall it as near-verbatim, but confirm the exact sentence before quoting it on a slide. 'Dual' / 'comparative' means two separate blocks at the same target using two local anesthetics of different expected duration (conventionally lidocaine then bupivacaine), with concordant short/long relief. ASIPP's position is that true placebo (saline) controls are not practical in routine practice, so comparative local anesthetic blocks are the accepted control. Note the deliberate two-part criterion: a pain-score drop alone is not sufficient — functional confirmation is required. Contrast with Cohen 2020 multispecialty, which is more permissive on both the number of blocks and the threshold (see separate finding) — this is a genuine live disagreement, not a settled point.

*원 출처 표기*: `Manchikanti L, Kaye AD, Soin A, et al. Pain Physician 2020;23(3S):S1-S127`

### [미확인] Recalled ASIPP 2020 evidence levels — REPORT WITH CAUTION, this is the single most error-prone item in my notes.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION and I am explicitly low-confidence on the specific grades. My recall: diagnostic lumbar facet joint nerve blocks = Level I or Level II; therapeutic lumbar medial branch blocks = Level II; lumbar radiofrequency neurotomy = Level II; lumbar intra-articular facet injections = Level III or IV (i.e., the weakest of the four, and ASIPP is notably unenthusiastic about IA injections). ASIPP uses its own modified grading (Level I = strong, II = moderate, III = fair, IV = limited, V = consensus/opinion) which is NOT the same scale as GRADE or USPSTF — do not silently convert. Because I cannot distinguish Level I from II for diagnostic blocks, or III from IV for IA injections, DO NOT put these grades on a slide without opening the guideline's summary table. This is exactly the kind of item where a wrong cell is a critical failure.

### [미확인] ASIPP frequency/repetition guidance differs in kind from payer rules: it is relief-driven, not calendar-driven.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION and I am low-confidence on the specific integers. Recalled principles: repeat therapeutic blocks are justified only when the prior block produced a defined benefit (commonly framed as >=50% relief for a minimum interval, on the order of 6-8 weeks or longer), repeat intervals should not be shorter than roughly 2 months, and an annual cap in the range of 4-6 procedures per region per year is applied. ASIPP has historically been more permissive than Medicare on therapeutic MBB specifically, because ASIPP recognizes therapeutic MBB as a treatment in its own right while Medicare largely does not — that payer/society divergence is itself worth a slide. Do not state the integers without opening the guideline.

*원 출처 표기*: `Manchikanti L et al., Pain Physician 2020;23(3S):S1-S127 (and the ASIPP 2013 predecessor)`

### [보통] Prevalence estimates for lumbar facet-mediated pain span 10% to 40%+, and the spread is driven almost entirely by the diagnostic criterion used — this belongs on the same slide as the threshold debate.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION. Low end: Cohen SP, Raja SN. 'Pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of lumbar zygapophysial (facet) joint pain.' Anesthesiology 2007;106(3):591-614 (PMID recalled 17325518 — verify) is the source I associate with the commonly quoted 10-15% figure. High end: Schwarzer AC et al., Ann Rheum Dis 1995;54(2):100-106 reported 40% (95% CI 27-53), and Manchikanti's group has reported ranges in the 27-40% band using dual blocks with an 80% criterion. The determinants of the spread: single vs dual blocks, 50% vs 80% threshold, referral population (primary care vs tertiary pain clinic), and age distribution. Do not present a single prevalence number as 'the' prevalence — presenting the range and its drivers is both more honest and more useful.

*원 출처 표기*: `Cohen SP, Raja SN. Anesthesiology 2007;106(3):591-614; Schwarzer AC et al. Ann Rheum Dis 1995;54(2):100-106; Manchikanti L et al. Pain Physician 2020;23(3S):S1-S127`
