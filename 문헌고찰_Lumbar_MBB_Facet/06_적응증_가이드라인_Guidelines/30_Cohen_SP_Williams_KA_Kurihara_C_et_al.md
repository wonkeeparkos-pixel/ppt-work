## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (2건)

### [미확인] Cohen 2010 is the counter-argument: it directly randomized the NUMBER of diagnostic blocks and found the 0-block strategy most cost-effective despite the lowest success rate.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION. Cohen SP, Williams KA, Kurihara C, et al. 'Multicenter, randomized, comparative cost-effectiveness study comparing 0, 1, and 2 diagnostic medial branch (facet joint nerve) block treatment paradigms before lumbar facet radiofrequency denervation.' Anesthesiology 2010;113(2):395-405 (PMID recalled 20613471 — verify). Recalled design: approximately 150 patients randomized to proceed to RFA with no block, after one positive block, or after two concordant blocks. Recalled success rates at 3 months: roughly 33% (0 blocks), 39% (1 block), 64% (2 blocks) — VERIFY these three numbers, they are the crux of the slide. The paradox and the teaching point: dual blocks produced by far the highest per-patient success rate, yet the 0-block paradigm was the most cost-effective per successful outcome, because the blocks themselves cost money and screening out non-responders wastes the cost of the blocks on patients who never get treated. I recall cost-per-successful-treatment figures in the several-thousand to ~$17,000 range favoring the 0-block arm but do NOT trust my recall of the specific dollar values — omit them unless verified. This paper is why the Cohen 2020 consensus refused to mandate a universal block count: the answer depends on what you are optimizing.

*원 출처 표기*: `Cohen SP, Williams KA, Kurihara C, et al. Anesthesiology 2010;113(2):395-405`

### [보통] The 50% vs 80% threshold trade-off, stated plainly: raising the threshold raises RFA success rate per treated patient but shrinks the eligible pool and raises cost per success.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION — this is the synthesis, and it is the framing I would build the pathway slide around. At >=80% dual-block criterion (ASIPP/SIS), reported RFA success is high (Dreyfuss 2000: 60% with >=90% relief at 12 months; Cohen 2010 dual-block arm: ~64% at 3 months) but many patients are screened out. At >=50% single-block criterion (used by MINT and by many European and cost-constrained systems), the treated population is larger and more heterogeneous and reported success rates fall toward the 33-39% range seen in Cohen 2010's 0- and 1-block arms. Neither threshold is 'correct' — they encode different utility functions (maximize per-patient benefit vs. maximize population throughput per dollar). ASIPP/SIS resolve this toward 80%; Cohen 2020 explicitly declines to resolve it; MINT used the permissive end and got a null result. Present all three positions.

*원 출처 표기*: `Synthesis of Dreyfuss P et al. Spine 2000;25(10):1270-1277; Cohen SP et al. Anesthesiology 2010;113(2):395-405; Cohen SP et al. Reg Anesth Pain Med 2020;45(6):424-467; Juch JNS et al. JAMA 2017;318(1):68-81`
