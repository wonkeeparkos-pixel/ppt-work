### [미확인] Item-by-item verdict on the traditional facet-pain checklist: which items have any support and which are pure folklore.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION; verdicts are my reading of the literature above, not a quotation. FOLKLORE / FAILED VALIDATION: 'axial pain worse with extension' and 'worse with extension-rotation' (Kemp sign) — failed in Schwarzer 1994 and scored inversely in Revel; 'relief with flexion/sitting' — same; 'morning stiffness' — no validation I can point to. WEAK BUT NOT ZERO: paraspinal tenderness — cited in the Cohen 2020 consensus as perhaps the most consistent single sign, and I recall Cohen SP, Hurley RW, et al., Clin J Pain 2007;23(1):45-52 ('Clinical predictors of success and failure for lumbar facet radiofrequency denervation') reporting paraspinal tenderness as associated with RFA success and longer pain duration / higher baseline opioid use with failure — VERIFY that paper's citation and direction of effect before using. OLDER AGE: supported as a prevalence modifier (higher facet-pain prevalence in older cohorts) and embedded in Revel as age >65 — but prevalence is not the same as predicting an individual's block response. OBESITY: associated with radiographic facet OA; I have no evidence it predicts block response and would not claim it. 'NO PAIN BELOW THE KNEE': associated with facet pain in Jackson 1988, but directly contradicted by provocation studies (Marks, Pain 1989, n=138/385 observations; Fukui, Clin J Pain 1997) which show distal referral including below the knee — so it is a soft prior at best, and must not be used as a rule-out. IMAGING FACET OA: not a predictor; presence on CT/MRI rises steeply with age (Kalichman L et al., Spine 2008, is the reference I recall for CT prevalence) and correlates poorly with pain.
