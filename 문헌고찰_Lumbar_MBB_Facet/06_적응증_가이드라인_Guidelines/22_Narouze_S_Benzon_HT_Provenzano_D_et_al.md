### [보통] ASRA/ESRA anticoagulation guidance: Narouze S, Benzon HT, Provenzano D, et al., Reg Anesth Pain Med 2018;43(3):225-262 (second edition) is the governing document.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION (PMID recalled as 29278603, DOI recalled as 10.1097/AAP.0000000000000700 — verify both). Full recalled title: 'Interventional Spine and Pain Procedures in Patients on Antiplatelet and Anticoagulant Medications (Second Edition): Guidelines From the American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine, the European Society of Regional Anaesthesia and Pain Therapy, the American Academy of Pain Medicine, the International Neuromodulation Society, the North American Neuromodulation Society, and the World Institute of Pain.' Its central methodological move — and the reason it matters for facet work — is that it abandoned one-size-fits-all holding and stratified interventional pain procedures into HIGH / INTERMEDIATE / LOW bleeding-risk tiers, with the tier driven mainly by whether a hematoma could compress neural tissue in a closed space. I did NOT confirm a third edition exists; check whether one has appeared, since the task window extends to 2026.

### [미확인] Facet MBB and intra-articular facet injection are classified as LOW bleeding-risk procedures, for which ASRA/ESRA 2018 does not require routine discontinuation of antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy — but I could not verify the table, and the placement of RFA is genuinely uncertain in my recall.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION — flagging carefully because the task prompt asserted the LOW-risk classification and I must not simply launder that assertion back as confirmed. My independent recollection does agree that facet MBB and IA facet injection sit in the LOW-risk tier, on the same footing as peripheral nerve blocks, peripheral/musculoskeletal joint injections, trigger point injections, and sacroiliac joint injection / sacral lateral branch blocks — the rationale being that these are extra-neuraxial targets where a hematoma has somewhere to go. The recalled recommendation for the low-risk tier is that antithrombotic therapy may be CONTINUED (aspirin and NSAIDs certainly; anticoagulants generally), with the decision individualized via shared decision-making with the prescribing physician and weighted toward thrombotic risk, since the recalled evidence base for these procedures is largely case-report level. ### [보통] Relative contraindications and patient-selection cautions.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION. (a) THERAPEUTIC ANTICOAGULATION / ANTIPLATELET THERAPY — relative only; per ASRA 2018 low-risk stratification these are generally continued for facet procedures (see the caveats in the ASRA finding above). (b) PREGNANCY — the issue is ionizing radiation from fluoroscopy, not the injection; elective blocks are deferred, and if a block is unavoidable, ultrasound-guided lumbar medial branch technique is the described radiation-free alternative (recalled reference: Greher M et al., Anesthesiology 2004;100(5):1242-1248, a sonoanatomic feasibility study — VERIFY; note also that Medicare has generally treated ultrasound guidance as non-covered for facet procedures, so the radiation-sparing option and the reimbursable option can conflict). (c) UNCONTROLLED DIABETES — corticosteroid causes transient hyperglycemia; the well-quantified data are for epidural steroid rather than facet injection, so I would not put a specific mg/dL or duration figure on a slide without a facet-specific source. Practical handling: consider a steroid-free diagnostic MBB (local anesthetic only — which is what a diagnostic block should be anyway), warn the patient, and arrange glucose monitoring. (d) IMMUNOSUPPRESSION — raises infection risk and argues for strict asepsis and steroid avoidance. (e) PSYCHOLOGICAL/PSYCHIATRIC COMORBIDITY — see separate finding; this is an evidence-supported modifier of outcome, not merely a soft caution. (f) ACTIVE SUBSTANCE USE DISORDER, unrealistic expectations, or a patient seeking a cure rather than a diagnostic answer. (g) ACTIVE COMPENSATION/LITIGATION — widely taught as a negative outcome predictor; I could not verify a facet-specific citation and would present it as clinical lore unless a source is found. (h) INABILITY TO LIE PRONE or to cooperate with post-block pain diary scoring — a genuinely underrated exclusion, because an uninterpretable diary makes the whole diagnostic exercise worthless.
