### [보통] Suggested slide architecture for the indications/selection section, given that the honest evidence story is mostly negative.

Editorial, not a literature finding — offered because the parent is building a teaching deck and this topic's evidence base resists a tidy summary. A structure that survives an expert audience: (1) 'Who do you even test?' — the five gates (3 months, failed conservative care, axial not radicular, no unaddressed red flags, imaging to exclude). (2) 'The exam won't tell you' — Jackson 1988, Schwarzer 1994/1995, Revel 1998 vs Laslett 2004, Maas 2017; make the point that Revel scores mechanical provocation in the OPPOSITE direction to what is taught at the bedside. (3) 'So the block is the test' — false-positive rate ~38% single block, hence dual comparative blocks; volume discipline and no sedation as validity requirements. (4) 'How strict?' — the 50% vs 80% trade-off with Dreyfuss 2000 and Cohen 2010 side by side, presented as a utility choice rather than a right answer. (5) 'MINT and its critics' — present the null result and the three critiques without adjudicating. (6) 'What you are allowed to do' — ASIPP relief-driven repetition vs Medicare calendar caps, and the divergence between them. (7) 'Who not to inject' — absolute vs relative contraindications, with the ASRA low-risk classification as the reassuring item (MBB is often the procedure of choice in the anticoagulated patient). The deck's existing content_mbb.py already ends at the clinical-predictor table, so section (1) here follows directly from its last slide.
