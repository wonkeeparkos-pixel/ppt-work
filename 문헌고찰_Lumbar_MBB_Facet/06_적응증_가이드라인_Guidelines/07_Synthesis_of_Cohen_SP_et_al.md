### [보통] Composite indication set for diagnostic lumbar MBB, as it recurs across ASIPP / NASS coverage policy / Medicare LCDs — the five gates.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION; this is a synthesis of the recurring elements, not a quotation from any one document. (1) DURATION: chronic axial low back pain of at least 3 months, moderate-to-severe (LCDs commonly operationalize as >=3/10 or a stated functional limitation). (2) CONSERVATIVE CARE: documented failure of a trial of non-interventional management — the recalled range across policies is 6 weeks to 3 months, typically requiring more than one modality (e.g., physical therapy or a directed home exercise program PLUS pharmacotherapy such as NSAIDs/acetaminophen). NASS coverage policy is the one I recall as specifying ~6 weeks; several LCDs use 3 months. Report the range, not a single number. (3) PAIN CHARACTER: predominantly axial/paraspinal, NOT radicular — absence of clinical radiculopathy or neurogenic claudication, and no untreated neurological deficit. (4) NO UNADDRESSED RED FLAGS / no surgically remediable pathology: infection, fracture, tumor, cauda equina, progressive deficit must be excluded or already managed. (5) IMAGING: recent imaging required to exclude the above — explicitly NOT to demonstrate facet arthropathy, which is neither necessary nor sufficient. VERIFY the specific week/month thresholds against the current policy documents; they differ by payer and have been revised.

### [미확인] Item-by-item verdict on the traditional facet-pain checklist: which items have any support and which are pure folklore.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION; verdicts are my reading of the literature above, not a quotation. FOLKLORE / FAILED VALIDATION: 'axial pain worse with extension' and 'worse with extension-rotation' (Kemp sign) — failed in Schwarzer 1994 and scored inversely in Revel; 'relief with flexion/sitting' — same; 'morning stiffness' — no validation I can point to. WEAK BUT NOT ZERO: paraspinal tenderness — cited in the Cohen 2020 consensus as perhaps the most consistent single sign, and I recall Cohen SP, Hurley RW, et al., Clin J Pain 2007;23(1):45-52 ('Clinical predictors of success and failure for lumbar facet radiofrequency denervation') reporting paraspinal tenderness as associated with RFA success and longer pain duration / higher baseline opioid use with failure — VERIFY that paper's citation and direction of effect before using. OLDER AGE: supported as a prevalence modifier (higher facet-pain prevalence in older cohorts) and embedded in Revel as age >65 — but prevalence is not the same as predicting an individual's block response. OBESITY: associated with radiographic facet OA; I have no evidence it predicts block response and would not claim it. 'NO PAIN BELOW THE KNEE': associated with facet pain in Jackson 1988, but directly contradicted by provocation studies (Marks, Pain 1989, n=138/385 observations; Fukui, Clin J Pain 1997) which show distal referral including below the knee — so it is a soft prior at best, and must not be used as a rule-out. IMAGING FACET OA: not a predictor; presence on CT/MRI rises steeply with age (Kalichman L et al., Spine 2008, is the reference I recall for CT prevalence) and correlates poorly with pain.

*원 출처 표기*: `Synthesis of Cohen SP et al. Reg Anesth Pain Med 2020;45(6):424-467; Cohen SP, Hurley RW et al. Clin J Pain 2007;23(1):45-52; Jackson RP et al. Spine 1988; Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37-40; Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303-307`

### [보통] Relative contraindications and patient-selection cautions.

UNVERIFIED IN-SESSION. (a) THERAPEUTIC ANTICOAGULATION / ANTIPLATELET THERAPY — relative only; per ASRA 2018 low-risk stratification these are generally continued for facet procedures (see the caveats in the ASRA finding above). (b) PREGNANCY — the issue is ionizing radiation from fluoroscopy, not the injection; elective blocks are deferred, and if a block is unavoidable, ultrasound-guided lumbar medial branch technique is the described radiation-free alternative (recalled reference: Greher M et al., Anesthesiology 2004;100(5):1242-1248, a sonoanatomic feasibility study — VERIFY; note also that Medicare has generally treated ultrasound guidance as non-covered for facet procedures, so the radiation-sparing option and the reimbursable option can conflict). (c) UNCONTROLLED DIABETES — corticosteroid causes transient hyperglycemia; the well-quantified data are for epidural steroid rather than facet injection, so I would not put a specific mg/dL or duration figure on a slide without a facet-specific source. Practical handling: consider a steroid-free diagnostic MBB (local anesthetic only — which is what a diagnostic block should be anyway), warn the patient, and arrange glucose monitoring. (d) IMMUNOSUPPRESSION — raises infection risk and argues for strict asepsis and steroid avoidance. (e) PSYCHOLOGICAL/PSYCHIATRIC COMORBIDITY — see separate finding; this is an evidence-supported modifier of outcome, not merely a soft caution. (f) ACTIVE SUBSTANCE USE DISORDER, unrealistic expectations, or a patient seeking a cure rather than a diagnostic answer. (g) ACTIVE COMPENSATION/LITIGATION — widely taught as a negative outcome predictor; I could not verify a facet-specific citation and would present it as clinical lore unless a source is found. (h) INABILITY TO LIE PRONE or to cooperate with post-block pain diary scoring — a genuinely underrated exclusion, because an uninterpretable diary makes the whole diagnostic exercise worthless.
