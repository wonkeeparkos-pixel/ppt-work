### [보통] Two distinct targets exist — the mid-joint line and the INFERIOR RECESS — and the inferior recess is the more forgiving and more commonly recommended entry.

The lumbar ZJ has a superior (superolateral) subcapsular recess and an inferior (inferomedial) subcapsular recess, both of which balloon beyond the articular margins and both of which are larger and more distensible than the mid-joint cleft. MID-JOINT-LINE entry: needle passed down-the-beam through the profiled cleft in the oblique view; requires the joint to be genuinely open on that projection and fails in advanced degenerative joints with osteophytic bridging or a vacuum-narrowed cleft. INFERIOR-RECESS entry: needle directed in a near-AP or minimally oblique projection to the inferior pole of the joint, walking off the inferior margin of the inferior articular process; the recess is entered from below and the needle tip sits in a capsular pouch rather than between cartilage surfaces. The inferior recess is the standard fallback for degenerated joints and for L5-S1, and it is generally taught as the first-choice target for therapeutic IA injection. The SUPERIOR recess is intentionally avoided as a primary target because it lies closest to the ligamentum flavum/epidural space and to the intervertebral foramen — it is the recess whose rupture spills into the epidural space.
