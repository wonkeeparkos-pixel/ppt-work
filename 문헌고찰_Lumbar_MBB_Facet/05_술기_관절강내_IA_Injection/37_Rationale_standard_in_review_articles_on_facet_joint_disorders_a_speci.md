## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [미확인] Special indication 2 — INFLAMMATORY ARTHROPATHY AND ACTIVE SYNOVITIS/EFFUSION: steroid must reach the synovium, which only the intra-articular route achieves.

Situations where the pathology is intrinsically synovial rather than nociceptive-signalling: spondyloarthropathies (ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis) with facet involvement; rheumatoid arthritis; crystal arthropathy (CPPD/gout) of the facet joint; and acute facet SYNOVITIS/EFFUSION seen as T2-hyperintense fluid distending the joint on MRI (the 'facet fluid sign', itself associated with degenerative spondylolisthesis and segmental instability). The mechanistic argument for the deck: a medial branch block or RFA interrupts afferent signalling but leaves an inflamed synovium untreated, whereas intra-articular corticosteroid acts on the inflamed tissue itself — so the presence of demonstrable synovitis/effusion is the cleanest indication to choose IA over MBB. Also in this category: septic facet arthritis, where the intra-articular route is used for DIAGNOSTIC ASPIRATION and culture (steroid contraindicated). The claim that an MRI facet effusion predicts a better response to IA steroid is plausible and is asserted in review articles, but I could not verify a specific predictive study.
