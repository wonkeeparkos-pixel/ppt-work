## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (2건)

### [미확인] 'Dorsal approach' = needle passed posteroanteriorly, roughly perpendicular to skin, puncturing the posterior capsule rather than threading the articular cleft.

Rationale: the dorsal segment of the curved joint is the most sagittal and is covered by a true fibrous capsule (the ventral capsule is replaced by ligamentum flavum). A posteroanterior needle therefore reliably meets fibrous capsule and can be pushed through it into the joint even when the cleft is not profiled. This is the approach that most tolerates the near-AP projections used at L5-S1 and in degenerated joints. Trade-off: the operator loses the down-the-beam confirmation of being in the cleft and depends entirely on the capsular endpoint plus the arthrogram to prove intra-articular position.

### [보통] Endpoint is a firm capsular 'pop'/give followed by immediate loss of the ability to advance, with rubbery-then-bony resistance.

Sequence of tactile cues: dense paraspinal fascia, then a firm rubbery resistance as the needle engages the fibrous capsule, then a distinct give/pop as the capsule is pierced, then hard resistance if the tip contacts articular cartilage or subchondral bone. Standard corrections: if bone is struck without a pop, the tip is on the dorsal cortex of an articular process — walk medially/laterally 1-2 mm in small steps; after a pop, withdraw ~1 mm before injecting so the orifice is not abutting cartilage. In a heavily degenerated joint the pop may be absent entirely, which is exactly why arthrographic confirmation is mandatory and tactile feel alone is not accepted as proof of intra-articular placement.
