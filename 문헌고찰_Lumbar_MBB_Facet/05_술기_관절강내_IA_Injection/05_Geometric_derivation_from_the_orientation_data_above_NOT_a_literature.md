## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [확실] Geometric rule for obliquity: required C-arm rotation from AP ≈ 90° minus the joint's angle from the sagittal plane — so MORE sagittal joints need MORE obliquity, and L5-S1 (most coronal) needs the LEAST.

This is a derivation, not a citation. The joint space opens when the x-ray beam lies within the joint plane. If the joint plane makes angle θ with the sagittal plane, an oblique view rotated α degrees from AP puts the beam (90° − α) from sagittal; setting 90° − α = θ gives α = 90° − θ. A purely sagittal joint (θ=0) therefore opens on a true lateral (α=90°); a purely coronal joint (θ=90°) opens on a true AP (α=0°). Because θ INCREASES caudally, the obliquity α required to profile the joint DECREASES caudally. This predicts: upper lumbar joints need the greater obliquity and L5-S1 needs a near-AP or minimally oblique view. In practice you never reach the geometrically ideal angle at L1-3 (you would need 55-65°, which is impractical and puts the needle path through paraspinal muscle bulk), so operators work with a compromise obliquity and target the recesses instead of the mid-joint line.
