## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (2건)

### [확실] Lumbar zygapophysial joints rotate progressively from near-sagittal at the thoracolumbar junction to markedly oblique/coronal at L5-S1.

The consistent, uncontested anatomic finding is the GRADIENT, not the exact degrees: upper lumbar joints (L1-2, L2-3) lie close to the sagittal plane, and the angle subtended with the sagittal plane increases at each successive caudal level, so L5-S1 is the most coronally oriented lumbar facet joint. Functional corollary used in teaching: sagittal orientation resists axial rotation but poorly resists anterior translation (hence degenerative spondylolisthesis is commonest at L4-5, the level where sagittalization plus tropism is most often found), while the coronal L5-S1 orientation resists the shear generated by the lumbosacral angle. This gradient is the single anatomic fact that drives all the level-specific C-arm obliquity differences.

### [보통] Typical working fluoroscopic sequence: prone with abdominal pillow, ipsilateral oblique to profile the joint, coaxial needle to the inferior recess.

Steps as conventionally taught: (1) prone, pillow/bolster under the lower abdomen to flatten lumbar lordosis and open the joints; (2) true AP with squared endplates of the target segment (cephalocaudal tilt until the endplate is a single line); (3) rotate the C-arm ipsilaterally in small (5°) increments until the target joint cavity is best profiled as a lucent cleft between the inferior articular process of the level above and the superior articular process of the level below; (4) skin wheal over the down-the-beam target; (5) 22G or 25G 3.5-inch (90 mm) spinal needle — a 5-inch (127 mm) needle for high BMI; a 25G needle is often preferred for the joint because it deflects into the cleft more forgivingly, a 22G for control at depth; (6) advance strictly coaxial ('tunnel vision'/gun-barrel) until bone or capsule is contacted; (7) confirm depth on lateral; (8) contrast, then injectate. Needle gauge and length are conventional practice figures, not trial-derived.
