## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [보통] The lumbar facet joint is CURVED (C- or J-shaped in the axial plane), not planar — the anterior third is coronal and the posterior two-thirds progressively sagittal.

This is why no single C-arm obliquity ever 'opens' the whole joint: the fluoroscopic 'joint space' you profile is the posterior (dorsal) segment only. Practical consequences for the deck: (1) the needle enters the dorsal, more sagittally oriented portion of the joint, which is why a dorsal approach works at all; (2) the anterior coronal segment of the joint abuts the ligamentum flavum and the epidural space, which is the anatomic basis for superior-recess rupture emptying into the epidural space; (3) at L5-S1 the whole joint is shifted toward coronal, so its dorsal segment is far less sagittal than at L2-3 and the entry vector changes accordingly.
