### [미확인] SOURCES CONFLICT on L5-S1 obliquity — report both positions rather than one.

POSITION A (geometry-driven, the majority position in interventional texts): because L5-S1 is the most coronal joint, it is profiled with the LEAST obliquity — often 0-20° from AP, sometimes with a caudocephalad or cephalocaudad tilt to clear the sacral ala and iliac crest; the inferior recess is entered essentially down-the-beam from a near-AP projection. POSITION B (the framing in the task prompt and in some procedural manuals): L5-S1 is described as requiring the MOST oblique projection and/or a distinct approach. These can be partially reconciled: L5-S1 is genuinely the hardest level and does often need 'a different approach' — but the difficulty is mechanical (overhanging iliac crest, deep joint, steep lumbosacral angle, sacral ala) rather than a need for extreme obliquity, and several manuals quote 30-45° for L5-S1 because they are describing the SCOTTIE-DOG view used for medial branch blocks, not the joint-profiling view used for IA entry. I could not verify which manual says which. Do not assert a single number for L5-S1 in the deck; state the geometric principle and note the practical obstacles.
