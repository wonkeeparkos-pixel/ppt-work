## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [미확인] Ultrasound accuracy has been validated mainly by CT control, with high success in cadavers/normal-BMI subjects and clearly degraded performance in obesity and at L5-S1.

The validation study most often cited for ULTRASOUND-GUIDED LUMBAR FACET INJECTION under CT control is Galiano K et al., Anesthesia & Analgesia, 2005 ('Ultrasound guidance for facet joint injections in the lumbar spine: a CT-controlled feasibility study'), which reported high correct-placement rates in a cadaver/feasibility setting. For the MEDIAL BRANCH analogue, Greher M et al., Anesthesiology, 2004 validated a sonographic approach against CT, also with high accuracy in cadavers. The obesity limitation is the point that matters clinically: a fluoroscopically controlled feasibility study in obese patients (Rauch S et al., Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine, 2009) found materially lower success for ultrasound-guided lumbar medial branch targeting in high-BMI patients. L5-S1 is the level most often reported as sonographically inaccessible (depth, overhanging iliac crest, steep sacral slope). I COULD NOT VERIFY THE ACTUAL ACCURACY PERCENTAGES OR SAMPLE SIZES for any of these — do not quote a percentage without opening the papers.
