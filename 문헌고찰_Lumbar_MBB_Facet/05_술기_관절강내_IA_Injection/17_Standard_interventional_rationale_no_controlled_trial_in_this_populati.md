## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (2건)

### [보통] CT guidance gives near-perfect placement and is preferred when the anatomy defeats fluoroscopy or when the target is a cyst or a deep/distorted joint.

Accepted CT indications: severe degenerative hypertrophy/osteophytic bridging with an unopenable cleft; scoliosis or transitional lumbosacral anatomy; prior posterior instrumentation or fusion where hardware obscures fluoroscopic landmarks and the joint above the construct is the target; morbid obesity; targeting a JUXTAFACET SYNOVIAL CYST for distension/rupture or aspiration; suspected septic facet arthritis where aspiration for culture is the goal; and research protocols requiring documented needle position. CT was also the guidance modality in several of the therapeutic trials (notably the hyaluronic-acid-vs-steroid work). Trade-offs to state on the slide: higher radiation dose than a well-run fluoroscopic case, no real-time contrast dynamics unless CT-fluoroscopy is used, and no live vascular-uptake detection.

*원 출처 표기*: `Standard radiology/interventional practice; CT guidance used in Fuchs S et al., Journal of Vascular and Interventional Radiology, 2005 and in the percutaneous synovial-cyst literature`

### [미확인] Special indication 3 — POST-SURGICAL / INSTRUMENTED ANATOMY: hardware destroys the medial branch landmarks, so the joint itself becomes the more identifiable target.

After posterior instrumented fusion, pedicle screws and rods obscure the SAP/transverse-process junction fluoroscopically and the medial branch course may be surgically disrupted, making a reliable medial branch block difficult or uninterpretable. The classic clinical problem is the symptomatic ADJACENT SEGMENT above a fusion, where the mobile facet joint above the construct is the pain generator; that joint is often still enterable intra-articularly, frequently under CT guidance to navigate hardware artifact and altered anatomy. Same reasoning applies after laminectomy/decompression with altered posterior element anatomy, and in severe scoliosis or transitional lumbosacral segments where medial branch landmarks are unreliable. This is a rationale-based, not trial-based, indication.
