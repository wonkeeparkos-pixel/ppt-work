## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (2건)

### [보통] 0.5 mL produces aberrant epidural/foraminal spread in a meaningful minority; a lower (more caudad, mamillo-accessory) needle position reduces it.

CT study in healthy volunteers: contrast spread to the epidural space and intervertebral foramina in 16% of injections with 0.5 mL contrast. Dreyfuss compared a needle-end position at the UPPER edge of the TP-SAP intersection with one MIDWAY between the upper border of the medial TP and the mamillo-accessory ligament: with the more caudad (mamillo-accessory) position, LOWER rates of spread to the ventral epidural space and spinal nerves were observed at the same 0.5 mL volume. Radiographic contrast incorporated the medial branches of the dorsal rami in EVERY injection (i.e., the block is reliable at the nerve; the problem is over-spread, not under-coverage). This is the direct technical argument for targeting slightly caudad within the notch rather than at its very superior lip.

### [보통] Causes of FALSE POSITIVES and how to avoid them: placebo effect, excessive superficial local anesthetic, inappropriate sedation, and aberrant/excessive spread of injectate.

Documented causes: (1) placebo response — mitigated by dual/comparative blocks and by requiring ≥80% relief WITH the ability to perform previously painful movements; (2) 'excessive administration of superficial anesthetic' — infiltrating a large skin/subcutaneous wheal anesthetizes cutaneous branches and produces relief unrelated to the medial branch; keep the skin wheal minimal (~0.5 mL of 2% lidocaine subdermally is what is described in protocols) and place it away from the painful area; (3) 'the inappropriate use of sedation'; (4) 'aberrant flow of local anesthetic' — controlled by injecting ≤0.25-0.5 mL, avoiding a too-superior needle position, and confirming spread pattern with live-fluoro contrast.
