## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (7건)

### [확실] Target anatomy: the L1-L4 medial branch runs in the groove at the junction of the superior articular process (SAP) and the transverse process (TP) of the vertebra BELOW, then is bound to bone by the mamillo-accessory ligament (MAL).

The medial branch of the lumbar dorsal ramus courses close to the root of the posterolateral side of the SAP of the level below. Opposite the caudal border of the zygapophysial joint it turns medially through the groove between the mamillary and accessory processes, where it is held tight by the MAL. The MAL bridges the mamillary and accessory processes of each lumbar vertebra and encloses the medial branch in an osseofibrous tunnel; this tunnel maintains the proximal course of the medial branch in a CONSTANT relationship to bone — the anatomic basis for a bony-contact endpoint. The MAL is a fibrous cord ~1–2 mm thick and is more developed at L1–L4 than at L5. From L1 to L4, each medial branch travels between the SAP and TP of the vertebral level below (e.g., the L1 medial branch crosses between the L2 SAP and L2 TP).

### [보통] Patient position and C-arm sequence: prone, start true AP, then rotate the C-arm IPSILATERALLY to the oblique 'Scotty dog' view.

Patient prone under the fluoroscope. An AP view is obtained first; the image intensifier is then rotated obliquely (ipsilateral to the target side) to bring the SAP-TP junction into profile and produce the 'Scotty dog'. Endplate squaring by cranio-caudal tilt is universal practice to obtain a true AP at the level of interest (eliminating endplate parallax so the TP/SAP relationship is not foreshortened), but I could NOT find a primary source stating the degree of tilt or explicitly citing the rationale — treat the 'why' as accepted practice rather than a cited number.

*원 출처 표기*: `Wheeless' Textbook of Orthopaedics, Section 12 Ch 3 (Pain Management Procedures); technique descriptions in Amrhein TJ et al., AJR 2016, PMID 27276532`

### [보통] AP-view confirmation: needle tip should lie right at, or slightly MEDIAL to, the lateral margin of the SAP, at the level of the superior border of the TP.

'In the AP view, needle position is confirmed with the needle tip located right at or slightly medial to the lateral margin of the superior articular process.' The medial branch lies at the level of the TP or slightly superior and lateral to the medial border of the SAP. The needle is directed to the junction between the SAP and the superior border of the TP. A tip that projects lateral to the SAP margin in AP is too lateral (on the TP body/off the notch); a tip well medial to it risks the interlaminar/epidural direction.

*원 출처 표기*: `Technique descriptions synthesized in WikiMSK, Lumbar Medial Branch Blocks; Amrhein TJ et al., AJR 2016, PMID 27276532`

### [확실] Needle: 22-25 G, 3.5-6 inch (89-150 mm) spinal needle; most commonly a 22-G 3.5-inch curved Quincke-tip (22-G 5-inch for larger habitus); 25-G hypodermic for skin/soft-tissue anesthesia first.

'A 22- to 25-gauge, 3.5- to 6-inch spinal needle is advanced under fluoroscopic guidance to target levels at the junction of the medial aspect of the transverse processes and the superior articular processes.' More specifically a '22-gauge, 3.5-inch (or 22-gauge, 5-inch needle for patients with larger body habitus) curved Quincke-tip spinal needle' after initial skin anesthesia with a 25-G hypodermic needle. The bevel curve is used to steer the tip into the notch. For the L5 dorsal ramus a 25-G spinal needle is described.

*원 출처 표기*: `Amrhein TJ et al., Technique for CT Fluoroscopy-Guided Lumbar Medial Branch Blocks and Radiofrequency Ablation, AJR 2016, PMID 27276532; Musculoskeletal Key oblique-approach chapter`

### [보통] Skin entry and coaxial ('down-the-beam') technique: because the target sits behind the Scotty-dog 'eye', the skin puncture is placed directly in line with the beam and the needle is advanced coaxially until bone is contacted.

'The target point is located behind the eye of the Scotty dog, so the puncture site on the skin is directly in line using a coaxial technique under fluoroscopy.' Practical description: 'Insert spinal needle slightly lateral to target, go down the beam, and advance until bone is contacted.' The needle hub should obscure the shaft (a single dot) on the image. Caveat from the RFA literature: with steep angles of needle declination, 'greater angles of needle declination can make placement using a coaxial view (down the barrel) impractical in many cases' — relevant when the iliac crest forces a shallow oblique. Confirmation is then done sequentially in AP, ipsilateral oblique and lateral planes.

*원 출처 표기*: `The Procedure Guide, Fluoroscopic Guided LMBB; WikiMSK, Lumbar Medial Branch Blocks; Amrhein TJ et al., AJR 2016, PMID 27276532`

### [보통] CT / CT-FLUOROSCOPY guidance: same anatomic target, used when precision or soft-tissue avoidance matters.

'The dorsal ramus of the spinal nerve gives rise to the medial branch, which courses along a notch between the superior articular process and transverse process, with the target point for medial branch blocks and radiofrequency ablation located within this notch IMMEDIATELY PROXIMAL TO THE MAMILLOACCESSORY LIGAMENT.' Rationale for CT fluoro: 'allows more-precise needle tip positioning', 'real-time visualization on cross-sectional images, resulting in a superior ability to avoid adjacent soft-tissue structures and to achieve more-precise needle placement'. Practical indications (inferred from that rationale rather than stated as a list): distorted or fused post-surgical anatomy, severe facet hypertrophy/osteophyte obscuring the notch, high iliac crest at L5-S1, obesity defeating both fluoro landmarks and ultrasound, and operators already working in a CT suite. Needle advanced in AP/oblique/lateral planes until the tip is perpendicular to the mammillary process at the vertebral body and/or sacral ala.

*원 출처 표기*: `Amrhein TJ et al., Technique for CT Fluoroscopy-Guided Lumbar Medial Branch Blocks and Radiofrequency Ablation, AJR Am J Roentgenol 2016, PMID 27276532, doi:10.2214/AJR.15.15694; Value of CT fluoroscopy for lumbar facet blocks, PMID 15891151`

### [보통] Practical oblique workflow summary for the deck (L1-L4), synthesized from the technique sources.

1) Prone, arms above head or at sides, pillow under abdomen to reduce lordosis. 2) True AP; square endplates with cranio-caudal tilt at the level of interest. 3) Rotate C-arm ipsilaterally until the SAP-TP inflexion is profiled and the pedicle is sharply delineated — typically 10-25°, patient-specific, up to ~35-40° in some anatomies. 4) Identify the Scotty dog; the target is the ear-nose junction (SAP-TP), at the superior-dorsal margin of the pedicle shadow, above the mamillo-accessory notch and lateral to the mamillary process. 5) Skin wheal with 1-2% lidocaine via 25 G, minimal volume (~0.5 mL), placed coaxially. 6) 22-25 G, 3.5-5 inch curved Quincke spinal needle down-the-beam to bony contact in the notch. 7) Confirm AP (tip at/just medial to lateral SAP margin, at superior TP border), oblique (in the notch), and lateral if depth or foraminal entry is in question. 8) Inject 0.1-0.3 mL non-ionic contrast under LIVE fluoroscopy — look for a bony-hugging blush, reject vascular runoff. 9) Inject ≤0.25-0.5 mL local anesthetic per nerve, no steroid for a diagnostic block. 10) Repeat for the second nerve; for two adjacent joints block 3 nerves. 11) Post-procedure pain diary over the expected anesthetic duration.
