## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [확실] Target anatomy: the L1-L4 medial branch runs in the groove at the junction of the superior articular process (SAP) and the transverse process (TP) of the vertebra BELOW, then is bound to bone by the mamillo-accessory ligament (MAL).

The medial branch of the lumbar dorsal ramus courses close to the root of the posterolateral side of the SAP of the level below. Opposite the caudal border of the zygapophysial joint it turns medially through the groove between the mamillary and accessory processes, where it is held tight by the MAL. The MAL bridges the mamillary and accessory processes of each lumbar vertebra and encloses the medial branch in an osseofibrous tunnel; this tunnel maintains the proximal course of the medial branch in a CONSTANT relationship to bone — the anatomic basis for a bony-contact endpoint. The MAL is a fibrous cord ~1–2 mm thick and is more developed at L1–L4 than at L5. From L1 to L4, each medial branch travels between the SAP and TP of the vertebral level below (e.g., the L1 medial branch crosses between the L2 SAP and L2 TP).
