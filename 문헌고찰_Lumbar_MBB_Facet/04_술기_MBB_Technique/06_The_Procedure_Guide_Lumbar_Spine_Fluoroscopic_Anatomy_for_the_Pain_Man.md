## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (5건)

### [보통] SOURCES DISAGREE on the degree of ipsilateral oblique rotation. Reported values span 10° to ~40°, clustering at 15–25°.

Reported ranges found: (a) 'optimal Scotty Dog view usually occurs at 10 to 20 degrees'; (b) 'the image intensifier is rotated obliquely through 15° in order to visualize the target point'; (c) 'placing the electrode at a 20 degrees ipsilateral oblique angle to the sagittal plane toward the junction of the SAP and TP' (traditional RFA/MBB approach); (d) 'the C-arm typically has a 25-35° oblique angulation'; (e) one technique source states the inflexion point between the SAP and TP is usually brought into profile 'at about 40 degrees'. Practical rule that all sources agree on: obliquity is titrated to the individual patient until the SAP-TP inflexion point is sharply profiled and the pedicle is sharply delineated, NOT set to a fixed number. I could not verify a level-specific L1-L4 vs L5 degree prescription from a primary source; at L5/S1 the standard teaching is that oblique views are of limited value (see L5 dorsal ramus finding).

*원 출처 표기*: `Wheeless' Textbook of Orthopaedics (15°); The Procedure Guide, Fluoroscopic Guided LMBB (10-20°); Musculoskeletal Key / Radiology Key, Lumbar Zygapophysial Joint Nerve Injection—Oblique Approach; WikiMSK Lumbar Medial Branch Blocks (~40° inflexion point)`

### [확실] Scotty dog landmarks: eye = PEDICLE, nose = transverse process, ear = superior articular process, neck = pars interarticularis, front leg = inferior articular process, body = lamina.

The 'eye' of the Scotty dog is the pedicle seen en face. This is a critical teaching point because the medial branch target is NOT the eye itself — the eye/pedicle is the transpedicular target. The medial branch target is the junction where the 'ear' (SAP) meets the 'nose' (TP), which projects at the superior-dorsal margin of the pedicle shadow. Descriptions in the literature are terminologically inconsistent: some texts say the target is 'just superior to the eye', others 'behind the eye of the Scotty dog', and others describe the needle tip as sitting 'at the superior border of the pedicle isthmus and below the junction of the superior articular and transverse processes'. All are describing the same SAP-TP notch.

*원 출처 표기*: `The Procedure Guide, Lumbar Spine Fluoroscopic Anatomy for the Pain Management Interventionalist; Musculoskeletal Key, Lumbar Zygapophysial Joint Nerve (Medial Branch) Injection—Oblique Approach`

### [확실] TRUE oblique target point (L1-L4): the junction of the SAP and TP, at the point where the nerve crosses MIDWAY between the superior border of the TP and the mamillo-accessory notch.

The classic SIS-style description: 'For the L1 through L4 medial branches, the target needle destination is at the junction of the superior articular process and the transverse process, where the target nerve crosses midway between the superior border of the transverse process and the mamilloaccessory notch.' The needle is inserted on the oblique view aiming at the pedicle, ensuring the tip is ABOVE the mamillo-accessory notch and LATERAL to the mamillary process. Endpoint is bony contact in the notch.

*원 출처 표기*: `Musculoskeletal Key / Radiology Key, Lumbar Zygapophysial Joint Nerve (Medial Branch) Injection—Oblique Approach; The Procedure Guide, Fluoroscopic Guided LMBB`

### [보통] Skin entry and coaxial ('down-the-beam') technique: because the target sits behind the Scotty-dog 'eye', the skin puncture is placed directly in line with the beam and the needle is advanced coaxially until bone is contacted.

'The target point is located behind the eye of the Scotty dog, so the puncture site on the skin is directly in line using a coaxial technique under fluoroscopy.' Practical description: 'Insert spinal needle slightly lateral to target, go down the beam, and advance until bone is contacted.' The needle hub should obscure the shaft (a single dot) on the image. Caveat from the RFA literature: with steep angles of needle declination, 'greater angles of needle declination can make placement using a coaxial view (down the barrel) impractical in many cases' — relevant when the iliac crest forces a shallow oblique. Confirmation is then done sequentially in AP, ipsilateral oblique and lateral planes.

*원 출처 표기*: `The Procedure Guide, Fluoroscopic Guided LMBB; WikiMSK, Lumbar Medial Branch Blocks; Amrhein TJ et al., AJR 2016, PMID 27276532`

### [보통] Practical oblique workflow summary for the deck (L1-L4), synthesized from the technique sources.

1) Prone, arms above head or at sides, pillow under abdomen to reduce lordosis. 2) True AP; square endplates with cranio-caudal tilt at the level of interest. 3) Rotate C-arm ipsilaterally until the SAP-TP inflexion is profiled and the pedicle is sharply delineated — typically 10-25°, patient-specific, up to ~35-40° in some anatomies. 4) Identify the Scotty dog; the target is the ear-nose junction (SAP-TP), at the superior-dorsal margin of the pedicle shadow, above the mamillo-accessory notch and lateral to the mamillary process. 5) Skin wheal with 1-2% lidocaine via 25 G, minimal volume (~0.5 mL), placed coaxially. 6) 22-25 G, 3.5-5 inch curved Quincke spinal needle down-the-beam to bony contact in the notch. 7) Confirm AP (tip at/just medial to lateral SAP margin, at superior TP border), oblique (in the notch), and lateral if depth or foraminal entry is in question. 8) Inject 0.1-0.3 mL non-ionic contrast under LIVE fluoroscopy — look for a bony-hugging blush, reject vascular runoff. 9) Inject ≤0.25-0.5 mL local anesthetic per nerve, no steroid for a diagnostic block. 10) Repeat for the second nerve; for two adjacent joints block 3 nerves. 11) Post-procedure pain diary over the expected anesthetic duration.
