## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [보통] Sedation: evidence is mixed but converges on 'sedation does not invalidate the block IF strict criteria (≥80% relief + restored painful movement) are used'.

Manchikanti's controlled work found as many as 10% of patients reported significant relief (≥80%) with the ability to perform prior painful movements after perioperative sodium chloride, midazolam, or fentanyl — i.e., false positives can be produced in a small proportion — yet concluded that 'IV sedation with fentanyl or midazolam does not alter the diagnostic validity of controlled comparative local anesthetic blocks in the cervical or lumbar spine' when the ≥80%-plus-function standard is applied. A 2024 observational study found midazolam did not change patients' perceived pain intensity following MBB, nor false-positive rates after RFA. Counter-position: a randomized controlled crossover study of sedation and the accuracy/treatment outcomes of diagnostic injections exists and should be read before teaching this as settled (PMID 24524866).
