## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (3건)

### [보통] Reported lumbar false-positive rates vary widely across the literature — 21-33% in one summary vs 49.8% (95% CI 42.7-56.8%) in another from the same group. Report both.

One summary states false-positive rates of single local anesthetic blocks are 27-63% cervical, 42-58% thoracic, and 21-33% LUMBAR. A separate figure from the same body of work gives a lumbar facet joint pain false-positive rate of 49.8% (95% CI, 42.7%, 56.8%) using controlled comparative local anesthetic blocks (1% lidocaine first, then 0.25% bupivacaine if an appropriate response was obtained). These are not reconciled in the material I could access — the discrepancy likely reflects different relief thresholds (≥50% vs ≥80% with restoration of previously painful movements) and different populations.

*원 출처 표기*: `Manchikanti L et al., Low Back Pain and Diagnostic Lumbar Facet Joint Nerve Blocks: Assessment of Prevalence, False-Positive Rates, and a Philosophical Paradigm Shift from an Acute to a Chronic Pain Model, Pain Physician 2020, PMID 32967394`

### [미확인] Comparative (dual) blocks judged by duration of anesthesia yield specificity 88% but sensitivity only 54% against placebo control.

'Comparative medial branch blocks considering duration of anesthesia tested against placebo yielded a specificity of 88%, but only a marginal sensitivity of 54%.' Teaching implication: comparative blocks are a rule-IN test — they protect against operating on false positives at the cost of denying nearly half of genuine facet-pain patients. This also sits in tension with the newer finding that lidocaine/bupivacaine durations are not reliably concordant.

*원 출처 표기*: `Attributed within Manchikanti/Bogduk review literature (Pain Physician 2020, PMID 32967394); original placebo-controlled validation attributable to Bogduk/Lord methodology — primary source not directly verified`

### [보통] Causes of FALSE POSITIVES and how to avoid them: placebo effect, excessive superficial local anesthetic, inappropriate sedation, and aberrant/excessive spread of injectate.

Documented causes: (1) placebo response — mitigated by dual/comparative blocks and by requiring ≥80% relief WITH the ability to perform previously painful movements; (2) 'excessive administration of superficial anesthetic' — infiltrating a large skin/subcutaneous wheal anesthetizes cutaneous branches and produces relief unrelated to the medial branch; keep the skin wheal minimal (~0.5 mL of 2% lidocaine subdermally is what is described in protocols) and place it away from the painful area; (3) 'the inappropriate use of sedation'; (4) 'aberrant flow of local anesthetic' — controlled by injecting ≤0.25-0.5 mL, avoiding a too-superior needle position, and confirming spread pattern with live-fluoro contrast.

*원 출처 표기*: `Manchikanti L et al., Pain Physician 2020, PMID 32967394; Dreyfuss P et al., Spine 1997, PMID 9127924; Wahezi SE et al., PM R 2018, PMID 29174073`
