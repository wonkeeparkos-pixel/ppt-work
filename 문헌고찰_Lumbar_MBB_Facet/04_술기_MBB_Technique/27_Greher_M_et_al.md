## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (3건)

### [확실] US target and needling: transverse view, IN-PLANE lateral-to-medial, 45-60° to skin, 20-22 G spinal needle, target = the groove at the cephalad margin of the TP adjacent to the SAP; then rotate back to longitudinal to confirm.

'The target point is the groove at the cephalad margin of the transverse process adjacent to the superior articular process.' Under real-time guidance with an in-plane technique a 20-22 G spinal needle is inserted lateral to medial at approximately 45°-60° to the skin (a 22-G 10 cm Quincke is specified in one protocol for the axial in-plane approach). After bony contact, rotate the probe 90° back to the longitudinal view to verify the tip sits on the cephalad aspect of the TP — the two-plane check substitutes for the fluoroscopic AP/lateral pair. Greher's original description used a paravertebral CROSS-AXIS view and a LONG-AXIS view defined at 15 MHz, with block needles guided at 2-6 MHz.

*원 출처 표기*: `ASRA Pain Medicine blog (2019); Greher M et al., Ultrasound-guided lumbar facet nerve block: a sonoanatomic study of a new methodologic approach, Anesthesiology 2004;100:1242-1248, PMID 15114223`

### [확실] US LIMITATION 1 — BMI. Success falls with obesity; sources DISAGREE on whether obesity is an absolute barrier.

Numbers: 'The correct target was reached in 72.5% of procedures overall, but in 80.5% of procedures on patients with BMI <30.' Patients with BMI > 35 kg/m2 are commonly EXCLUDED from US studies because of poor visualization; resolution decreases with the depth of the fat layer. DISAGREEMENT: Greher et al. found ultrasound landmarks of adequate quality even in a subject with BMI 36 kg/m2 (landmarks 'good in 19 and of sufficient quality in one' of 20 volunteers) and therefore regarded obesity as a NON-absolute contraindication; Rauch et al., in a dedicated fluoroscopically confirmed feasibility study in obese patients, concluded that medial branch blocks CANNOT reliably be performed under ultrasound guidance in obese patients. Report both.

*원 출처 표기*: `Greher M et al., Anesthesiology 2004;100:1242-1248, PMID 15114223; Rauch S et al., Ultrasound-Guided Lumbar Medial Branch Block in Obese Patients: A Fluoroscopically Confirmed Clinical Feasibility Study, Reg Anesth Pain Med 2009; Ashmore Z et al., Pain Reports 2022, PMC9113209`

### [보통] Practical ultrasound workflow summary for the deck.

1) Prone, pillow under abdomen. 2) Curvilinear 2-5 MHz probe, parasagittal 3-4 cm lateral to midline; obtain the TRIDENT sign (successive TP domes with acoustic shadows); count levels from the sacrum upward, mark midpoints. 3) Rotate 90° to axial: identify spinous process, lamina, facet joint, SAP, TP. 4) Target = groove at the cephalad margin of the TP where it meets the SAP. 5) 20-22 G spinal needle (22 G 10 cm for deeper patients), IN-PLANE lateral to medial at 45-60° to skin, to bony contact. 6) Rotate probe back to longitudinal to confirm the tip lies on the cephalad aspect of the TP. 7) Inject small volume, watching for the fluid to layer against bone. 8) For L5 dorsal ramus: transducer over the S1 SAP / sacral ala; needle inserted just caudad to probe midpoint, advanced caudocephalad to the S1-ala junction; oblique OUT-OF-PLANE in a rotated cross-axis view is the validated variant; verify in longitudinal view that the tip has not passed the ala into the L5/S1 foramen. 9) Accept that intravascular uptake cannot be detected — prefer fluoroscopy for diagnostic blocks where a false negative matters.
