## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [확실] Each lumbar zygapophysial joint is DUALLY innervated — one painful joint requires TWO medial branch blocks; 3 nerves cover 2 adjacent joints.

To anesthetize one facet joint you must block the medial branch at that level and the one above. The L4-L5 joint is innervated by the L3 and L4 medial branches. Consequently, the standard practice is 2 medial branches per joint; to cover two adjacent joints (e.g., L4-L5 and L5-S1) you block 3 nerves — L3 MB, L4 MB, and the L5 dorsal ramus. A 2024 anatomical study of 80 medial branches found all L1–L4 medial branches gave off 1–2 small articular branches while crossing the facet joint, innervating the joint of the LOWER segment, and 19/80 (23.75%) gave recurrent branches innervating the joint of the UPPER segment — i.e., the dual-innervation model is anatomically confirmed with a recurrent-branch minority variant.
