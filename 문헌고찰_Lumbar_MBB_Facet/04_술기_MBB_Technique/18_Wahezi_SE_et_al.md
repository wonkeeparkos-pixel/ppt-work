## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (4건)

### [확실] INJECTATE VOLUME: current consensus is < 0.5 mL total per nerve; 0.25 mL is the volume that best matches the RFA lesion and preserves specificity.

2024 Consensus Guidelines: 'Lumbar medial branch blocks should be performed with < 0.5 mL (total volume) to reduce spread.' Cadaveric dose-finding (6 cadavers, bilateral fluoro-guided MBB via posterior oblique approach, 0.25 mL on one side vs 0.50 mL on the other, 9:1 Omnipaque 240 + 1% methylene blue): both volumes adequately coated the medial branches, but the 0.5 mL cohort showed consistent spread DORSALLY to superficial muscles and to distal segments of the dorsal branches distant from the target nerves, whereas 0.25 mL spread stayed contained in the deep and intermediate lumbar muscular layers close to the target. Conclusion: after a 0.25 mL block, RFA will contact everything the block anesthetized; after 0.5 mL, distal dorsal branches are anesthetized that RFA cannot ablate — a structural mechanism for block-positive/RFA-negative discordance.

### [확실] A 0.5 mL injectate may block the ADJACENT-level medial branch, degrading level specificity; 0.25 mL reliably contacts the target nerve without extensive extravasation.

'A 0.5-mL injectate volume in clinical practice may produce an adjacent-level nerve block in addition to the intended injection level, thus decreasing the specificity of a targeted lumbar medial branch block. In contrast, a 0.25-mL quantity of injectate reliably contacted the lumbar medial branches without extensive extravasation, and 0.25 mL total volume for a lumbar medial branch block may provide greater specificity for radiofrequency ablation planning.' Investigators explicitly hypothesized that injectate volume is a contributor to the high false-positive rate of diagnostic blocks.

### [보통] Causes of FALSE POSITIVES and how to avoid them: placebo effect, excessive superficial local anesthetic, inappropriate sedation, and aberrant/excessive spread of injectate.

Documented causes: (1) placebo response — mitigated by dual/comparative blocks and by requiring ≥80% relief WITH the ability to perform previously painful movements; (2) 'excessive administration of superficial anesthetic' — infiltrating a large skin/subcutaneous wheal anesthetizes cutaneous branches and produces relief unrelated to the medial branch; keep the skin wheal minimal (~0.5 mL of 2% lidocaine subdermally is what is described in protocols) and place it away from the painful area; (3) 'the inappropriate use of sedation'; (4) 'aberrant flow of local anesthetic' — controlled by injecting ≤0.25-0.5 mL, avoiding a too-superior needle position, and confirming spread pattern with live-fluoro contrast.

*원 출처 표기*: `Manchikanti L et al., Pain Physician 2020, PMID 32967394; Dreyfuss P et al., Spine 1997, PMID 9127924; Wahezi SE et al., PM R 2018, PMID 29174073`

### [보통] Practical oblique workflow summary for the deck (L1-L4), synthesized from the technique sources.

1) Prone, arms above head or at sides, pillow under abdomen to reduce lordosis. 2) True AP; square endplates with cranio-caudal tilt at the level of interest. 3) Rotate C-arm ipsilaterally until the SAP-TP inflexion is profiled and the pedicle is sharply delineated — typically 10-25°, patient-specific, up to ~35-40° in some anatomies. 4) Identify the Scotty dog; the target is the ear-nose junction (SAP-TP), at the superior-dorsal margin of the pedicle shadow, above the mamillo-accessory notch and lateral to the mamillary process. 5) Skin wheal with 1-2% lidocaine via 25 G, minimal volume (~0.5 mL), placed coaxially. 6) 22-25 G, 3.5-5 inch curved Quincke spinal needle down-the-beam to bony contact in the notch. 7) Confirm AP (tip at/just medial to lateral SAP margin, at superior TP border), oblique (in the notch), and lateral if depth or foraminal entry is in question. 8) Inject 0.1-0.3 mL non-ionic contrast under LIVE fluoroscopy — look for a bony-hugging blush, reject vascular runoff. 9) Inject ≤0.25-0.5 mL local anesthetic per nerve, no steroid for a diagnostic block. 10) Repeat for the second nerve; for two adjacent joints block 3 nerves. 11) Post-procedure pain diary over the expected anesthetic duration.
