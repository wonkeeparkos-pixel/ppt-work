## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [확실] The L5 dorsal ramus is anatomically unique: it is much LONGER than L1-L4, has NO lateral branch, and it is the PARENT TRUNK (not a medial branch) that lies on bone at the target site.

The L5 dorsal ramus arises just outside the L5-S1 foramen and runs dorsally at the bottom of a groove bounded medially by the S1 superior articular process and caudally/laterally by the ala of the sacrum. It gives off its medial branch only when it reaches the CAUDAL edge of the S1 SAP / caudal aspect of the L5-S1 joint; the medial branch then wraps medially around the posterior aspect of the L5-S1 joint and terminates in multifidus. The L5 dorsal ramus forms only a medial and an intermediate branch — it lacks a lateral branch because iliocostalis has no attachment at L5 (replaced by the iliolumbar ligament). Consistent with this, the dorsal-ramus bifurcation distance at L5 (1.87 ± 0.88 mm from the superior border of the TP root) is roughly half that at L1-L4.
