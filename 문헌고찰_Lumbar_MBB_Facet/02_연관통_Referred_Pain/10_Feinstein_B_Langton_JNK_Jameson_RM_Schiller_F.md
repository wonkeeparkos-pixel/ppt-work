### [미확인] How far distally does facet pain refer? Buttock and thigh dominate; below-knee referral is uncommon but is documented as far as the foot. I could NOT verify any reliable PROPORTION.

Consistent qualitative statements across reviews: referred pain is 'typically around the buttock and thigh and is rarely felt below the knee'; 'pain referred distal to the knee is infrequently associated with facet pathology'; but 'radiating referred pain below the knee as far as the foot has also been reported.' Where a below-knee facet-mediated presentation is explained mechanistically, reviews invoke facet SYNOVIAL CYST causing true root compression rather than somatic referral. Fukui's 6-zone scheme did not include any below-knee zone, so that study cannot supply a percentage. I searched specifically for a numeric proportion (Schwarzer, Fukui, Marks, DePalma, RFA cohorts) and found NONE that I can cite honestly. DO NOT put a below-knee percentage on a slide without reading the primary text. Related classic datum that supports 'deep somatic tissue CAN refer past the knee': Kellgren showed noxious stimulation of interspinous ligaments produced referred pain extending as far as the foot — but conversely Feinstein 1954 reported that with paravertebral 6% saline 'pain could NOT be induced in the radial aspect of the upper limbs and in the FEET.' Sources disagree; report both.

### [확실] WHY the maps overlap — mechanism 3: sclerotomal (deep somatic) rather than dermatomal organisation, established by the classic 1939–1954 experiments.

Kellgren JH (Clin Sci 1939;4:35–46) injected 6% hypertonic saline into interspinous ligaments and muscles in 3 subjects, mapping segmental deep-pain areas from below C4 to the sacrum: pain from muscle was felt DIFFUSELY and referred on a SPINAL SEGMENTAL pattern; interspinous ligaments gave the same. Inman VT & Saunders JB (J Nerv Ment Dis 1944;99:660–667) confirmed and extended this using both clinical skeletal pathology and hypertonic-saline/needling of periosteum, ligament and tendon in volunteers. Feinstein B et al. (J Bone Joint Surg Am 1954;36(5):981–997, PMID 13211692) is the largest and most decisive: paravertebral 6% saline injections from occiput to sacrum, FIVE subjects per intervertebral level. Verbatim results: the distributions 'were found to approximate a segmental plan, although they OVERLAPPED CONSIDERABLY and DIFFERED IN LOCATION FROM THE CONVENTIONAL DERMATOMES'; sympathetic and somatic plexus blocks did NOT interfere with the referred pain, 'suggesting a primarily spinal integrative mechanism'; and — opposite to Head's zones — areas of HYPO-algesia overlay the induced deep pain. TEACHING LINE: sclerotomal referral is segmental in principle but overlapping and non-dermatomal in practice; expecting a crisp dermatome-like lumbar facet map is a category error. All four are CLASSIC (pre-1990).
