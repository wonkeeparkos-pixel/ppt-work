## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (4건)

### [확실] Marks 1989 — the single most quotable evidence that facet referral is NOT sclerotomal and NOT segmental: 385 observations in 138 patients, no consistent pattern.

Diagnostic spinal infiltration of lumbar facet joints and related structures, comparing JOINT infiltration with NERVE (medial branch) infiltration. Key numbers and results: (1) In 385 observations on 138 patients, NO consistent segmental or sclerotomal pattern was found. (2) GROIN pain was produced from L2 all the way to L5 — i.e. groin referral is NOT specific to upper lumbar levels. (3) Pain radiating to the BUTTOCK or TROCHANTERIC region occurred mostly from the L4 and L5 levels. (4) Crucially: 'the nerves supplying the facet joints gave rise to distal referral of pain significantly more commonly than the joints themselves' — i.e. medial branch stimulation refers FURTHER distally than joint distension. This directly contradicts Fukui's medial-branch data (which was more lumbar-dominant than joint distension); present both positions rather than reconciling them. CLASSIC (1989).

*원 출처 표기*: `Marks R. 'Distribution of pain provoked from lumbar facet joints and related structures during diagnostic spinal infiltration.' Pain 1989;39(1):37–40`

### [확실] Fukui's own conclusion is the money quote for 'maps cannot localise the level': distributions were SIMILAR across levels and overlap was CONSIDERABLE.

Verbatim from the abstract: 'The distribution of referred pain from the L1/2 to L5/S1 zygapophyseal joints, and the medial branches of the dorsal rami from L1 to L5 were similar for each level stimulated, and the overlap of referred pain between each level was considerable.' Combined with Marks 1989 ('no consistent segmental or sclerotomal pattern' in 385 observations) and McCall 1979 ('overlap between the upper and lower lumbar spine'), all three independent provocation datasets converge on the same negative conclusion. This is the strongest evidence chain in the whole topic and should carry the teaching slide.

*원 출처 표기*: `Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810); Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37–40; McCall IW, Park WM, O'Brien JP. Spine 1979;4(5):441–446`

### [보통] Groin pain is NOT a reliable upper-lumbar marker — sources agree it arises from L2 through L5.

Marks 1989 (385 observations, 138 patients): 'groin pain was produced from L2 to L5.' Fukui 1997: groin is a listed referral zone for L3/4, L4/5 AND L5/S1 joints, and for the L2 and L3 medial branches — though it is the LEAST frequent zone at every level that produces it. Consequence: a patient with groin pain cannot be assumed to have an upper lumbar facet source; conversely groin pain does not exclude L5/S1. This is a commonly taught error worth explicitly correcting on a slide.

*원 출처 표기*: `Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37–40; Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810)`

### [보통] Direct contradiction to flag between two provocation studies: do medial branches refer MORE distally than the joints, or LESS?

MARKS 1989: 'the nerves supplying the facet joints gave rise to DISTAL referral of pain significantly MORE commonly than the joints themselves.' FUKUI 1997: medial branch stimulation was 'mainly' to the lumbar spinal region at every level L1–L4, with distal zones described as occurring only 'rarely' — a more PROXIMAL-dominant picture than the corresponding joint distension at L3/4–L5/S1. WINDSOR 2002: medial branch referral zones 'are DIFFERENT than those reported after injection of the lumbar Z-joint' without stating a direction. Methodological differences that may explain it: Marks used chemical infiltration of the nerve (which spreads to adjacent structures), Fukui used electrical stimulation at minimal threshold during RF denervation in symptomatic patients, Windsor used minimum-threshold electrical stimulation in asymptomatic volunteers. Present as an open question rather than resolving it.

*원 출처 표기*: `Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37–40; Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810); Windsor RE et al. Pain Physician 2002;5(4):347–353 (PMID 16886011)`
