## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [보통] Differential to teach alongside the map: upper lumbar / thoracolumbar junction (T12-L1) pathology refers to the ILIAC CREST, groin and lower abdomen via the cluneal nerves — Maigne's syndrome.

Thoracolumbar junction (Maigne's) syndrome: irritation of the thoracolumbar facet joints refers pain into the distribution of the cluneal nerves derived from T12, L1 and L2, giving local thoracolumbar backache with referred pain to the ILIAC CREST and BUTTOCK. The superior cluneal nerves (lateral branches of the dorsal rami of L1–L3) pierce the thoracolumbar fascia near the posterior iliac crest; the most medial nerve crosses the crest through a rigid osseo-aponeurotic orifice 7–8 cm from the midline where it can be compressed. The corresponding ventral ramus innervates the lower abdomen, groin and pubic area, producing PSEUDO-VISCERAL and lower abdominal pain that mimics renal, gynaecological or hernial pathology. Two variants are described: a 'central' variant (thoracolumbar junction syndrome) and a 'peripheral' variant (cluneal nerve entrapment). CLINICAL POINT: iliac-crest and groin pain in a patient being worked up for lumbar facet pain should prompt examination of T12-L1, a level almost never included in a standard L3/L4/L5 medial branch block set. Note this is MEDIAL-branch-adjacent but LATERAL-branch (cluneal) mediated — a different nerve, same dorsal ramus.
