## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (2건)

### [확실] Windsor et al. 2002 — electrical stimulation of lumbar medial branches in 8 healthy males produced tight, reproducible composite maps that DIFFER from zygapophysial-joint injection maps.

Method: fluoroscopically guided, MINIMUM-THRESHOLD electrical stimulation of the RIGHT L1, L2, L3, L4 medial branches of the posterior primary ramus and the RIGHT L5 dorsal ramus in each of 8 healthy volunteer males (n = 40 stimulations), no history of back pain. All subjects were BLINDED to the level being stimulated and drew the referral area on a human line drawing at the time of each stimulus. Results: 'All of the subjects' mapped referral sites coincided with each other, creating a well defined composite drawing' — i.e. inter-subject reproducibility was high in asymptomatic volunteers. Headline conclusion: 'These referral zones are DIFFERENT than those reported after injection of the lumbar Z-joint, which may have clinical and therapeutic implications.' Teaching point: nerve-stimulation maps and joint-distension maps are not interchangeable — you cannot use one to interpret the other. I could not retrieve the per-level zone descriptions or the threshold voltages; do not invent them.

### [보통] Direct contradiction to flag between two provocation studies: do medial branches refer MORE distally than the joints, or LESS?

MARKS 1989: 'the nerves supplying the facet joints gave rise to DISTAL referral of pain significantly MORE commonly than the joints themselves.' FUKUI 1997: medial branch stimulation was 'mainly' to the lumbar spinal region at every level L1–L4, with distal zones described as occurring only 'rarely' — a more PROXIMAL-dominant picture than the corresponding joint distension at L3/4–L5/S1. WINDSOR 2002: medial branch referral zones 'are DIFFERENT than those reported after injection of the lumbar Z-joint' without stating a direction. Methodological differences that may explain it: Marks used chemical infiltration of the nerve (which spreads to adjacent structures), Fukui used electrical stimulation at minimal threshold during RF denervation in symptomatic patients, Windsor used minimum-threshold electrical stimulation in asymptomatic volunteers. Present as an open question rather than resolving it.
