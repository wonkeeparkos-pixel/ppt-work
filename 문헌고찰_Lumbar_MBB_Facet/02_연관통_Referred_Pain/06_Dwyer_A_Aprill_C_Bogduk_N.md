## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (1건)

### [확실] CONTRAST WITH CERVICAL (Dwyer/Aprill/Bogduk): cervical facet maps ARE segmentally distinguishable; lumbar maps are not. This is the single cleanest slide-level contrast.

Dwyer A, Aprill C, Bogduk N (Spine 1990;15(6):453–457, PMID 2402682): C2-3 through C6-7 distended with contrast under fluoroscopy in 5 normal volunteers; 'Each joint produced a clinically distinguishable, CHARACTERISTIC pattern of pain', enabling pain charts 'valuable in determining the segmental location of symptomatic joints.' Validated in patients by Cooper G, Bailey B, Bogduk N (Pain Med 2007;8(4):344–353, PMID 17610457) using pain drawings from patients with CONTROLLED diagnostic blocks: C2-3 (36%) and C5-6 (35%) were the commonest symptomatic joints, then C6-7 (17%); patient-derived maps 'provide a more representative guide to the recognition of the segmental origin' than volunteer-derived maps. Fukui's group published the parallel cervical work (Fukui S et al., Pain 1996;68(1):79–83, 'Referred pain distribution of the cervical zygapophyseal joints and cervical dorsal rami'). BOTTOM LINE FOR THE DECK: cervical = level-specific maps of real clinical utility; lumbar = overlapping maps of essentially no localising value. Same investigators, same method, opposite conclusion.
