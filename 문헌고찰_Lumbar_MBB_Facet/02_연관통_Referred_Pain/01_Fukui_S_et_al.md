## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (7건)

### [확실] Fukui 1997 is the definitive level-by-level lumbar facet referral study: 71 joints + 91 medial branches in 48 chronic LBP patients, 6 predefined referral zones.

Design: pain clinics of Kanto Teishin Hospital and Hannan Central Hospital, March 1994–May 1996. Under fluoroscopy, zygapophysial joints L1/2→L5/S1 were distended with contrast medium; lumbar medial branches of the dorsal rami Th12→L5 were electrically stimulated during RF facet denervation. If injection/stimulation reproduced the patient's usual pain, the induced pain distribution was recorded. Totals studied: 71 joints and 91 medial branches in 48 patients. The induced pain was scored into SIX regions only: (1) lumbar spinal (paraspinal) region, (2) gluteal region, (3) trochanteric region, (4) lateral thigh region, (5) posterior thigh region, (6) groin region. CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL POINT for the deck: there is NO 'flank', NO 'iliac crest', NO 'knee' and NO 'below-knee' zone in Fukui's scheme — the map is censored at the distal thigh, so Fukui CANNOT be cited for below-knee referral frequency. Headline conclusion in the authors' own words: the distributions from L1/2–L5/S1 joints and from L1–L5 medial branches 'were similar for each level stimulated, and the overlap of referred pain between each level was considerable.'

*원 출처 표기*: `Fukui S, Ohseto K, Shiotani M, Ohno K, Karasawa H, Naganuma Y. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307. PMID 9430810`

### [보통] LEVEL → ZONE TABLE, Fukui 1997, intra-articular joint distension (zones listed in the paper's stated order of frequency).

L1/2 — pain distributed to the LUMBAR (paraspinal) REGION ONLY; no distal referral observed. L2/3 — ALWAYS lumbar region; RARELY referred to trochanteric, gluteal and lateral thigh regions. L3/4 — lumbar region, then referred to gluteal > lateral thigh > posterior thigh > trochanteric > groin. L4/5 — lumbar region, then gluteal > lateral thigh > trochanteric > posterior thigh > groin. L5/S1 — lumbar region, then gluteal > lateral thigh > posterior thigh > trochanteric > groin. NOTE THE GRADIENT: distal referral appears only from L3/4 downward and is essentially absent at L1/2; groin referral is the LEAST frequent zone at every level that produces it (L3/4, L4/5, L5/S1). NOTE THE OVERLAP: L3/4, L4/5 and L5/S1 hit the SAME five extra-lumbar zones — they differ only in rank order (L4/5 ranks trochanteric above posterior thigh; L3/4 and L5/S1 rank posterior thigh above trochanteric). That single rank swap is the ONLY thing distinguishing three adjacent levels — which is exactly why the maps cannot localise a level.

*원 출처 표기*: `Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810), as tabulated in secondary reviews incl. Korean Journal of Pain / epain.org 'Facet joint disorders: from diagnosis to treatment' and MDPI Life 2024;14(11):1480 'Lumbar Facet Joint Disease: What, Why, and When?'`

### [미확인] Discrepancy between sources on the L5/S1 zone ordering — report both.

Position A (the reading closest to the primary text, and the one I found quoted verbatim): 'L5-S1 facet joint pain was distributed into the lumbar spinal region, and was referred to the gluteal, lateral thigh, posterior thigh, trochanteric, and groin regions, in order of frequency.' Position B (a paraphrase found in a secondary review): L5/S1 refers to the same pattern as L4/5 (gluteal > lateral thigh > trochanteric > posterior thigh > groin) 'with the exception of the lumbar spine'. The practical difference is trivial (rank of trochanteric vs posterior thigh) but flag it rather than assert one. If you need the exact figure for a slide, the original Fig. in Clin J Pain 1997;13:303–307 must be reproduced.

*원 출처 표기*: `Conflicting secondary summaries of Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810)`

### [보통] LEVEL → ZONE TABLE, Fukui 1997, MEDIAL BRANCH electrical stimulation — referral is markedly MORE lumbar-dominant and LESS distal than joint distension at the same level.

L1 medial branch — mainly LUMBAR spinal region; RARELY gluteal region. L2 medial branch — mainly lumbar region; rarely gluteal, trochanteric, groin, lateral thigh. L3 medial branch — mainly lumbar region; rarely gluteal, trochanteric, groin, lateral thigh. L4 medial branch — mainly lumbar region; rarely gluteal, trochanteric, lateral thigh AND posterior thigh (posterior thigh appears only at L4). L5 (medial branch / L5 dorsal ramus) — I could NOT verify the reported zone from any accessible source; treat as a gap and do not fabricate. Teaching point: the medial-branch maps and the joint maps are 'similar for each level stimulated' per the authors, yet the MB maps are dominated by the paraspinal region — consistent with the MB also supplying multifidus, interspinous ligament and periosteum, not just the joint capsule.

*원 출처 표기*: `Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810), as summarised in secondary reviews`

### [확실] Fukui's own conclusion is the money quote for 'maps cannot localise the level': distributions were SIMILAR across levels and overlap was CONSIDERABLE.

Verbatim from the abstract: 'The distribution of referred pain from the L1/2 to L5/S1 zygapophyseal joints, and the medial branches of the dorsal rami from L1 to L5 were similar for each level stimulated, and the overlap of referred pain between each level was considerable.' Combined with Marks 1989 ('no consistent segmental or sclerotomal pattern' in 385 observations) and McCall 1979 ('overlap between the upper and lower lumbar spine'), all three independent provocation datasets converge on the same negative conclusion. This is the strongest evidence chain in the whole topic and should carry the teaching slide.

*원 출처 표기*: `Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810); Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37–40; McCall IW, Park WM, O'Brien JP. Spine 1979;4(5):441–446`

### [보통] Groin pain is NOT a reliable upper-lumbar marker — sources agree it arises from L2 through L5.

Marks 1989 (385 observations, 138 patients): 'groin pain was produced from L2 to L5.' Fukui 1997: groin is a listed referral zone for L3/4, L4/5 AND L5/S1 joints, and for the L2 and L3 medial branches — though it is the LEAST frequent zone at every level that produces it. Consequence: a patient with groin pain cannot be assumed to have an upper lumbar facet source; conversely groin pain does not exclude L5/S1. This is a commonly taught error worth explicitly correcting on a slide.

*원 출처 표기*: `Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37–40; Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810)`

### [보통] Direct contradiction to flag between two provocation studies: do medial branches refer MORE distally than the joints, or LESS?

MARKS 1989: 'the nerves supplying the facet joints gave rise to DISTAL referral of pain significantly MORE commonly than the joints themselves.' FUKUI 1997: medial branch stimulation was 'mainly' to the lumbar spinal region at every level L1–L4, with distal zones described as occurring only 'rarely' — a more PROXIMAL-dominant picture than the corresponding joint distension at L3/4–L5/S1. WINDSOR 2002: medial branch referral zones 'are DIFFERENT than those reported after injection of the lumbar Z-joint' without stating a direction. Methodological differences that may explain it: Marks used chemical infiltration of the nerve (which spreads to adjacent structures), Fukui used electrical stimulation at minimal threshold during RF denervation in symptomatic patients, Windsor used minimum-threshold electrical stimulation in asymptomatic volunteers. Present as an open question rather than resolving it.

*원 출처 표기*: `Marks R. Pain 1989;39(1):37–40; Fukui S et al. Clin J Pain 1997;13(4):303–307 (PMID 9430810); Windsor RE et al. Pain Physician 2002;5(4):347–353 (PMID 16886011)`
