## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (3건)

### [확실] Schwarzer's referral-population study is the anchor low-end prevalence figure: ~15% by double blocks.

176 consecutive chronic LBP patients, no prior lumbar surgery. Screening blocks with lignocaine, confirmatory blocks with 0.5% bupivacaine. 83/176 (47%) had a 'definite or greater' response to the initial lignocaine block at one or more levels, but only 26/176 (15%) had >=50% response to the confirmatory bupivacaine block. This 26/176 = 15% is the prevalence estimate carried into the companion Spine 1994 paper ('Is the lumbar facet syndrome a clinical entity?', PMID 8059268), which found no clinical feature (history/exam, including Fairbank and Helbig-Lee criteria) predicted a positive confirmatory block.

*원 출처 표기*: `Schwarzer AC, Aprill CN, Derby R, Fortin J, Kine G, Bogduk N. Pain 1994;58(2):195-200. PMID 7816487; companion: Schwarzer AC et al. Spine 1994;19(10):1132-7. PMID 8059268`

### [확실] False-positive rate of a single uncontrolled lumbar MBB = 38%; positive predictive value only 31% (Schwarzer, the classic figure).

Using the confirmatory bupivacaine block as criterion standard in the same 176-patient cohort: false-positive rate of uncontrolled (single) diagnostic blocks = 38%; PPV = 31%. The PPV is internally consistent with the raw counts (26 confirmed / 83 lignocaine-positive = 31%). Interpretation: roughly 2 of every 3 patients who 'pass' a single block would be treated on a false premise.

*원 출처 표기*: `Schwarzer AC, Aprill CN, Derby R, Fortin J, Kine G, Bogduk N. 'The false-positive rate of uncontrolled diagnostic blocks of the lumbar zygapophysial joints.' Pain 1994;58(2):195-200. PMID 7816487`

### [확실] The comparative-block design substitutes a pharmacologic discriminator (duration) for a placebo arm: lidocaine first, bupivacaine second, positive only if relief is concordant and longer with the long-acting agent.

Standard Manchikanti protocol: 1% lidocaine as the screening block; responders re-blocked with 0.25% bupivacaine; positive = concordant relief on both, with the bupivacaine block producing longer relief, per IASP criteria. Schwarzer's protocol used lignocaine then 0.5% bupivacaine with a >=50% relief threshold on the confirmatory block. The design's advantage is ethical/practical feasibility (no deception, no saline arm); its acknowledged weakness is that a patient with a durable placebo response, or one who simply reports what is expected, can still pass both blocks.
