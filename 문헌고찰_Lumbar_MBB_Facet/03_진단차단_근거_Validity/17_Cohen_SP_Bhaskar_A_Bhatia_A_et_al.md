## 이 문헌에서 인용된 근거 (3건)

### [보통] Volume recommendations conflict: consensus guideline says <0.5 mL total; some technique references still teach 0.5-0.75 mL.

Cohen et al. 2020 multispecialty consensus: lumbar medial branch blocks should be performed with <0.5 mL total volume to reduce spread to adjacent structures. Against this, technique sources retrieved in this session still describe injecting 0.5-0.75 mL of 0.5% bupivacaine or 2% lidocaine slowly (with the caution to inject slowly to avoid ventral spread toward the DRG). The cadaveric data (Wahezi) push lower still, to 0.25 mL. Present this as an unresolved gradient: 0.25 mL (specificity-optimal in cadaver) vs <0.5 mL (guideline) vs 0.5-0.75 mL (legacy practice).

### [확실] The 50% vs 80% threshold is the single most contested parameter — and the two major guideline bodies disagree outright.

Cohen et al. state plainly that 'the cut-off designating an MBB as positive is one of the most controversial areas in pain medicine.' Positions: (a) Spine Intervention Society / IPSIS endorse DUAL blocks with an >=80% relief cut-off, on the grounds that RFA trials selecting patients this way show the highest positive-outcome rates; (b) the 2020 multisociety international consensus (Cohen et al.) recommends a SINGLE block with >=50% relief as the cut-off, on cost-effectiveness and access grounds; (c) ASIPP requires >=80% concordant relief on comparative blocks PLUS the ability to perform previously painful movements.

*원 출처 표기*: `Cohen SP, Bhaskar A, Bhatia A, et al. Reg Anesth Pain Med 2020;45(6):424-67 (PMID 32245841); Spine Intervention Society practice guidelines; ASIPP facet guidelines (Manchikanti L et al., Pain Physician guideline series)`

### [확실] MBB is the preferred diagnostic/prognostic test over intra-articular injection; consensus guideline states MBB is more predictive than IA.

The 2020 multispecialty international consensus, which addressed 17 topical questions including the diagnostic and prognostic value of MBB vs IA injections, concluded that lumbar medial branch RFA may benefit well-selected individuals, with MBB being more predictive than IA injections. Face-validity argument: the MBB tests exactly the neural target that RFA will lesion, whereas an IA injection tests the joint but not the nerve that will be ablated. Note the counterpoint from FACTS: the IA and MBB responder rates were statistically indistinguishable from each other (51% vs 56%), so the superiority of MBB rests on specificity and target-concordance arguments more than on head-to-head effect size.
