## 확인하지 못한 항목 (gaps)

- TOOLING LIMITATION: WebFetch returned HTTP 403 for every domain attempted (PubMed, PMC, EuropePMC, ScienceDirect, ASRA, ASIPP, even Wikipedia), and Bash curl through the proxy was blocked. All findings below are derived from WebSearch result summaries only — no primary full text or abstract was read directly. The 200-search budget was exhausted before all items could be cross-checked.
- Manchikanti 2004 (BMC Musculoskelet Disord 5:15) LUMBAR prevalence and false-positive figures were never surfaced. Only cervical (55%, 95% CI 49-61%) and thoracic (42%, 95% CI 30-53%) were retrieved. The commonly quoted lumbar pair of ~31% prevalence / ~27% false-positive is UNCONFIRMED.
- Manchikanti 2020 (Pain Physician 23:519-530) headline numbers of 34.1% prevalence / 49.8% false-positive rate came from a secondary summary, not the paper. Sample size, CIs, and criteria used are unknown. Must be verified before use.
- DePalma 2011 POST-FUSION study (Pain Med 12(5):732-9, PMID 21481166) — no numbers retrieved at all. The facet/SIJ/disc breakdown in the post-fusion population is a complete gap.
- Cohen 2010 cost-effectiveness: the per-successful-treatment dollar figures were not verifiable, nor were the group sizes for the 2-block arm or its success rate. Only the 0-block (17/51 = 33%) and 1-block (8/16 = 50%) success figures were confirmed.
- Cohen SP et al., Reg Anesth Pain Med 2015 (PMID 26066382), the multicenter case-control study directly comparing MBB vs IA injection as a prognostic tool before RFA — identified but no results retrieved. This is the single most relevant citation for section 7 and remains unquantified.
- Lord/Barnsley/Bogduk comparative-vs-placebo block validation (the '100% sensitivity, 65% specificity' figure) was NOT verified. It is cervical data in any case; no equivalent lumbar validation of comparative blocks against a placebo-controlled reference standard was located.
- Dreyfuss 1997 (Spine 22:895-902): only the 16% epidural/foraminal spread figure was retrievable via secondary citation. Sample size, number of injections, exact injectate, and the breakdown of spread patterns are unknown.
- No dedicated quantification of the PLACEBO response rate to saline medial branch blocks specifically was found. The 24% figure from FACTS is a randomized saline-arm responder rate at 3 months and is the best available proxy, but it is not a same-session placebo-block response rate.
- ASIPP guideline primary text (volume recommendation, exact wording of the 80% + functional-restoration criterion, and the source of the 34-48% prevalence / 42-58% false-positive ranges) could not be opened.
- Spine Intervention Society / IPSIS Practice Guidelines primary text (the specific recommended MBB volume — commonly cited as 0.3 mL, max 0.5 mL — and the formal 80% dual-block criterion wording) could not be verified.
- Bogduk N, F1000 Med Rep 2010;2:57 (the canonical defence of the double-block paradigm) and Dreyfuss 2000 Spine RFA outcomes (the '60% with >=90% relief at 12 months' figure) were both inaccessible. The pro-paradigm side of the debate is therefore documented far less precisely than the critique side.
- 2024 international survey of pain physicians on number of diagnostic blocks (PMID 39029928) — no response rates or practice-pattern percentages retrieved.
- No data retrieved on needle gauge, optimal MBB target point geometry, or the effect of sedation on false-positive rates (Manchikanti has a specific study on sedation as a confounder that was not located).
